LAWS Tracker

Americans for Responsible Innovation Design mock-up · V1 data

A research database of fielded kinetic weapon systems with autonomous targeting or engagement functions — what they are, who operates them, and exactly where the human sits in each system's kill chain.

The autonomy matrix

Every system, scored across 19 kill-chain functions ordered left to right as an engagement unfolds — from mission planning through weapon release and reattack. Darker cells mean more machine, less human. Read a row left to right to see where the human drops out. Click a row for the full system record.

Who builds, who operates

Tile shading shows how many of the 90 tracked systems each country operates. A dot marks countries that produce at least one tracked system. Click a country to open its systems in the database. (V1 mock-up uses a tile grid; the production site can swap in a full choropleth.)

Four decades of autonomy

Systems by first operational capability, grouped by inclusion tier. Supervised defensive autonomy (A2) has existed since the 1980s; the post-2020 surge sits almost entirely in expendable munitions with terminal autonomy (B1) and envelope-bound loitering weapons (A3). Hover a dot for the system; click for its record.

System database

All 90 systems. Filter by any dimension; click a row for the full record with autonomy profile, operators, and sources.

Methodology

The dataset covers fielded, kinetic-effect weapon systems with autonomy in target selection or engagement. Inclusion is tiered by where machine authority sits in the engagement sequence.

Coverage

Reviewed and excluded

Systems assessed but excluded from V1, with rationale — scope discipline is part of the method.

Data access

Every system record carries per-claim citations with source type and a confidence rating (29 of 90 systems fully sourced in V1; remainder in progress). Effect type is kinetic-only by scope; directed-energy and electronic-warfare systems are listed in the exclusion annex.
